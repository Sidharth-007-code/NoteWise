The Exponential Distribution & Memoryless Property

Prof. H. Vance · Discrete & Mathematical Statistics · schedule 18 min reading · Verified Folio

In probability theory, the exponential distribution represents the continuous analogue to the geometric distribution. It models the time elapsed between continuous, Poisson-governed occurrences that happen independently at a constant average rate denoted by λ > 0. Because of its tractability, it serves as the foundational pillar for queuing theory, operational risk assessment, and survival analysis.

Definition 3.1 — Probability Density Function Eq. 2.14
f(x; λ) = λ · e-λx   for   x ≥ 0
F(x; λ) = 1 - e-λx   (Cumulative Distribution Function)

Where λ denotes the event arrival rate parameter, and E[X] = 1/λ, with Var(X) = 1/λ².

A critical analytical property distinguishes the exponential law from all other continuous probability distributions: the absence of memory. Under this formulation, the remaining duration until an event occurs does not depend on how much time has already passed.

push_pin

"The memoryless property states that P(X > s + t | X > s) = P(X > t) for any non-negative s and t. In reliability engineering, this implies that an aged operational component is mathematically as good as new, suffering no cumulative wear or degradation."

Theorem 3.2 · Memoryless Formulation P(X > s+t ∩ X > s) / P(X > s)

To demonstrate this analytically, let X ~ Exp(λ). Then the survival function simplifies to S(t) = P(X > t) = e-λt. Applying the fundamental definition of conditional probability:

// Analytical proof of conditional invariance:

P(X > s + t | X > s) = P(X > s + t) / P(X > s)

= e-λ(s + t) / e-λs

= e-λs · e-λt / e-λs

= e-λt = P(X > t) ∎
